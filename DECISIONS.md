# Decisions

Choices made without stopping to ask. Newest last.

1. **Stack as suggested.** Next.js 15 (App Router) + TypeScript + Tailwind 4. Scripts run with `tsx`.
2. **Cache is committed.** `data/cache/cala/*.json` holds raw Cala responses keyed by a hash of (method, path, body). Headers (and so the API key) are never part of the key or the record. Committing it means the booth demo and re-ingests work offline and nothing is queried twice, even across machines.
3. **Node fetch behind a proxy.** In the cloud sandbox Node's built-in fetch needs `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1`. Documented in `.env.example`; no proxy code in the app.
4. **Fund resolution is automatic but audited.** Each seed fund is searched under several spellings; candidates must match an alias after `normalizeFund` (strips Fund I–VI, Ky, Oy, AB, LP, …), not be described as based in an unrelated country, and have investment relationships. Same-named strangers we caught by eye are listed in `seed.ts` (`exclude`, `excludeNames`). The full accept/reject log is `data/funds.json`.
5. **Portfolio = union of three link types**: `INVESTED_IN`, issuers of `PrivateCompanyFundingRound`s and `FundingRound`s the fund took part in, and the company-side `INVESTED_IN` (incoming). Fund-like targets (fund-of-funds links, e.g. Tesi into VC funds) are dropped.
6. **Company merging** is union-find over normalised names and aliases (≥ 4 chars), but never across two different countries ("Wolt" Finland vs "WOLT S.R.L." Italy stay apart).
7. **Client-side pacing at 1.6 req/s.** Cala returns 429 above roughly 1.5 req/s without rate-limit headers. The client paces requests, backs everyone off on a 429, and retries up to 6 times.
8. **Industrifonden dropped** from the seed list in practice: no matching investor entity found (see DATA_NOTES). Spintop Ventures replaces an uncertain name.
9. **"Concrete date" means day precision.** Month-only dates ("Oct 2026", "Early Nov 2026") and placeholders ("Recent") are never alerted on. A month-only query row is kept only if verification (graph round or a search claim) yields a day-precise date with a source.
10. **Doors "open" for 120 days.** A door's reason counts as "why now" for 120 days; the headline then narrows to this week / this month when it can.
11. **Door Count = doors with a real fit** (complement or same non-generic sector, fit score ≥ 2) per investor. Keyring size is kept alongside but not shown.
12. **LLM is optional.** With `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, claim extraction and fit lines use Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`, low effort, server-side refusal fallback on). Without it, regex + a sector lexicon with complement pairs. No key was available while building, so the shipped data uses the heuristics.
13. **Light only.** Cala's site has no dark theme; Faro matches it.
14. **Single input on the question screen.** The short description the spec mentions comes from Cala (or the demo profile), not a second field: "one input" won.
15. **Degrade, don't stop, on 402.** When Cala's balance ran out, the ingest switched to cache-only mode and builds minimal company records from what links and round descriptions already said. Nothing is invented; missing fields stay empty.
16. **Sector evidence from source titles.** With most descriptions missing or boilerplate, the fit heuristic also reads the titles and URL slugs of round sources *about that company* (aggregators and roundups skipped). It's evidence for picking a sector, never shown as text.
17. **A door's generic tags (AI, SaaS, data, consumer, marketing) never trigger a complement.** Too many companies mention AI; a complement needs a specific sector on the door's side.
18. **Demo startups: Oura, Lovable, Verda** (Lovable later replaced by RiACT, see 38). Real Nordic companies backed by seed funds (Lifeline + Tesi; byFounders + Creandum + Balderton + Antler; Lifeline + byFounders + J12 + Tesi), with several dated, sourced doors each. Their reports are frozen at `FARO_NOW=2026-11-14`.
19. **The scripted moment holds back the newest opened door** (Vensum for Verda and Oura) and reveals it on space, a click on "watch", or after `&auto=` ms.
20. **The map: investors as buildings, doors as windows.** Easier to read than a force graph at booth distance, and it fits the lighthouse metaphor. Up to 25 windows per building, lit first.
21. **Typed name wins for display.** "Oura" resolves to Cala's "OURA HEALTH OY" but reads as "Oura".
22. **Gmail add-on is a thin card** over `/api/doors/<startup>`; not tested in a live Gmail account.

## v2 (new north star, see BUILD_SPEC.md)

23. **v1 items reversed by the north star are listed in BUILD_SPEC.md** (people, subsidiaries, keyword fit, query endpoint, keyrings-as-doors, Slush). Nothing you changed during the session conflicted; the working tree was clean.
24. **The new Cala key lives in the environment's network secrets**, injected by the proxy into requests to api.cala.ai. Scripts run with `CALA_API_KEY=proxy-injected` (a placeholder the proxy overwrites). No key in any file.
25. **Door-centric, prioritised ingest.** The new key allows ~10 requests per rolling minute. Profiling all 2,989 Slush attendees plus 1,143 portfolio companies would take ~7 hours, so the ingest runs in priority order (users → fresh rounds → news → Slush attendees, Nordic legal names first), saves `data/pool.json` after each stage, and resumes from cache.
26. **"Matched by website", made workable.** Registry subsidiaries rarely carry a website, so a new national company is tied to its foreign parent by (a) a GLEIF parent link (`IS_DIRECT_PARENT_OF` / `IS_ULTIMATE_PARENT_OF`), rejected if both have websites and they differ; or (b) the same website or domain on a same-brand company headquartered abroad. Name similarity alone never counts.
27. **FILING founding date** = a founding-date source with `source_rank: FILING`, or from a known registry (GLEIF, PRH/YTJ, Bolagsverket, Brønnøysund, CVR, Companies House, INPI, Zefix…), since Cala's registry sources often arrive without a `source_rank`. Day precision only, and 1 January dates are treated as year placeholders.
28. **Windows:** a new national company counts as market entry for 365 days after its registry founding date; expansion news 180 days; fresh rounds 120 days.
29. **Why you is structured.** Industry codes are grouped into sectors by the code itself (`FINTECH` → finance, `MEDICAL_DEVICES` → health…). Customer = your sector sells to theirs; integration = products that connect (software–software, software–payments, software–health); distribution = same sector, and they're entering your market from abroad. Same code and same label = competitor, dropped. No overlap = no door.
30. **Slush match** by attendee id, or by the attendee's exact normalised name (attendees are listed under brand names while doors are often legal entities).
31. **Taps** live in a per-startup cookie on the web (`faro_taps_<slug>`) and in user properties in Gmail, with the same shape. Ranking runs client-side too, so a tap re-ranks instantly. "not now" hides that door and lowers its type; three "not now"s on a type mute it.
32. **First look = three doors, one per partnership type** where the data allows; after the first tap the log becomes the ranked list (up to six).
33. **Who:** CEO, CTO, VP and `WORKS_AT` roles (scraps like "per an exhibitor listing" filtered out), up to two people; otherwise a Google X-ray link whose role terms follow the partnership type. Link only.
34. **The map keeps its design**: investors behind warm paths are buildings, plus one building "New in <country>" for entrants without a warm path. Lit windows are the doors in the log.
35. **Door Count kept** (doors per investor via warm paths), as a quiet line. It doesn't conflict with v2.
36. **GLEIF parent links count as a match.** A new national company is tied to its foreign parent by a GLEIF parent link (`IS_DIRECT_PARENT_OF` / `IS_ULTIMATE_PARENT_OF`) or by the same website/domain; either is valid on its own. Name similarity alone never counts, however close. This refines the BUILD_SPEC line "not GLEIF parent links", which meant "not GLEIF alone as the discovery method"; the code (`foreignParent` and the entrants stage in `scripts/ingest.ts`) already accepts both.
37. **Industry codes that contradict the description.** `checkIndustries` (partner.ts) reads the door's Cala description, or, when that is boilerplate generated from the code ("Swedish fintech company founded in…"), the sourced Cala claim the door came from, against a short list of business names (data centre, bank, drones, software, chips, clinic, solar, logistics…; never activity words like "investment"). Silent or consistent: keep the codes. One clear sector the codes don't have: why-you uses it, and the door shows a † after the why-you line with a footnote "Cala codes it fintech; why-you read from its description (data centres). Uncertain." Several sectors, none the codes': unclear, drop. With no codes at all the description never creates a door (spec: no keyword guesses). Applied to entry doors and round doors.
38. **RiACT replaces Lovable as a demo startup.** The target was a seed-stage Nordic startup whose top door is tier 1 (entry + Slush + warm path). That isn't reachable with today's data: tiers 1 and 2 need the *entrant* at Slush, and none of the four (Cerebras, atNorth, Arondite, Tekever) attends in Cala, by id or by name. Warm paths are absent too: Arondite's only Cala investor (Creator 1 Fund) and Tekever's (NSSIF, Cedrus Capital, UK government) back 77 companies, none Norwegian or Danish (3 calls). The 24 Norwegian and Danish Slush attendees already profiled all reach Arondite or Tekever at tier 3 only, with no shared investor. Best available: **RiACT ApS** (Denmark, `SEED`, "software for robots", Slush 2026, backed by Butterfly Ventures, Navigare and Mølgaard Capital), whose door is Tekever → Denmark (6 Nov 2026, portugalresident.com) with customer, integration and distribution reasons and two named CEOs. Chosen over BatteryProof, Vyer Eco and Dalip (Norway, no Arondite fit as strong) and Limosa (defence batteries, but no stage or investors). Lovable's frozen report is removed; `/for/lovable` still works live from the pool. "The startup attends Slush" is shown nowhere as "meet them at Slush": that line stays reserved for doors that attend.
39. **Readable labels.** Cala's short labels (`robo`, `medtechPharma`, `dualUseTech`, `somethingElse`…) map to words ("robotics", "medtech", "dual-use tech", or the code's words) so why-you never says "will buy robo".
40. **Entrants pass order.** Foreign attendees next to the demo startups' industries go first: cached profile industries when present (health/medical/pharma/biotech and hardware/aerospace/defence/robotics score highest, internet/cloud next, software last), otherwise words in the attendee's name (health, med, robot, drone, defence, space, AI, cloud, data, chip…). Order only; it never decides a door. 378 of 2,777 scored above zero.
41. **Credit is reported, not guessed.** The client now counts what it actually sends (`calaStats`: sent, OK, status codes, cache hits), and the entrants stage writes `data/entrants-report.json` with those counts and an estimate of the calls left (attendees remaining × observed calls per attendee, at least one). Cala exposes no balance, so Faro states only what was spent and what remains.

## v2.1 (vision restated: Faro finds partners all year; Slush is one "where to meet" signal)

42. **Ranking follows the vision:** 1 market entry + warm path + shared event, 2 market entry + warm path, 3 market entry, 4 fresh round + warm path. A warm path now outranks an event: entry + Slush without an introducer is tier 3. The shared event is Slush 2026 (the event Cala has); the code reads `door.slush`, so a second event would plug in there.
43. **Gmail identity fallback.** The vision infers identity from the email domain. A personal address (gmail.com…) or a domain Cala doesn't know can't, so the add-on then asks for the startup's name once (the web's rule) and keeps it in user properties; "Change startup" (card link and ⋮ menu) resets it. Taps are stored per startup.
44. **Gmail add-on, production details.** Errors never crash the card (network failure, non-JSON answer, missing `FARO_URL` each get a quiet note); text is HTML-escaped; the tapped partnership type is the one shown; the hidden list is capped at 100 to stay inside user-property limits; `FARO_TEST_SENDER` (script property) simulates an email from a door, for testing only. Scope `gmail.addons.current.message.readonly` (the open message only), because `GmailMessage.getFrom()` needs it reliably. The API answers `{needsName}` with 200 instead of a 404, so the card can ask.
45. **Serverless-ready.** `next.config.ts` ships `data/pool.json`, `dataset.json` and the frozen demos with every function and leaves the 15 MB Cala cache out; cache writes are best effort, so a read-only filesystem never turns a good Cala answer into an error. Deploy target in GMAIL.md: Vercel, no env vars needed.
46. **Widened news, precision rules.** The first 50 widened searches kept 9 entries, and 5 were wrong: three wrong entities (CATL → a French bank "CATL GROUPE", Advantech → a UK retailer, Crane Worldwide Logistics → its Indian subsidiary), a joint venture written with a non-breaking hyphen, and a job ad as the source (Uber). New rules: the resolved company must match the claimed name exactly (name, legal name or alias), or carry the brand in its own website; it must have a home country; job boards and career pages are never sources; hyphen variants are normalised before the "not an entry" test. `--stage=recheck` re-applies the rules to stored news entries and overwrites the pool, so drops stick. "Country manager" appointments now count as an entry signal (Avanza → Denmark).
47. **More precision rules from the widened run.** A country-manager hire counts only when the claim, or a sentence of its cited text that isn't history (no year before 2025), says the presence is new (Avanza "establishing operations in Denmark": yes; SEB's new country manager in Finland, Amazon's in Sweden: no). One article gives one door (OpenAI and "OpenAI Group PBC" from the same story). A nationality in the claim must match the entity's country ("Ukrainian defence firm Fire Point" ≠ FIRE POINT S.R.L., Italy). "Partnering with" is a partnership, not an entry (Crusoe → Norway). `acquir\b` never matched "acquiring" (a word-boundary bug); buying a company is not an entry, buying land for a site is (atNorth → Norway). The heuristic now reads appositive leads ("Advantech, a Taiwanese … firm, opened…") and "reports that X opened…". Entries carry the cited text (`evidence`) so `--stage=recheck` judges them the same way later.
48. **Entity checks against the source.** When the article is the company's own press page (eip.com for "EIP"), the resolved entity must own that domain; otherwise it's a same-named stranger (EIP → a French ad agency). Incumbent phrasing ("expanding its largest European R&D hub", "moving into a new office", "doubling its local team") isn't entry (Databricks → Netherlands). The recheck now runs the same already-local test as ingest (headquartered there, or a same-brand company described as based there), so a replay can't re-add what the recheck drops. Same-named strangers caught by eye go in `MISRESOLVED` in `scripts/ingest.ts` with the reason, like `seed.ts` does for funds (ATHLETICA, a French retailer, isn't the Athletica in the Finland story). Run `--stage=recheck` on its own after any replay: stages run in a fixed order.
49. **Demo: three seed-stage startups with this week's market-entry doors.** Screened with `npm run screen` (all seed-stage Nordic startups in the pool, ranked by top tier, warm entry doors, entry doors, partnership variety). All candidates top out at tier 3: none shares an investor with an entrant. I fetched the entrants' VC portfolios (19 calls: Sequoia, Benchmark, Eclipse, SV Angel, Foundation, Coatue, Alpha Wave, Atreides, Vy, Tiger, Founders Fund, Thrive, Insight, Khosla, Point Nine, Galdana, Kleiner Perkins VIII, Creades) and found no seed-stage company in the market each is entering (Sigena AB, the one Swedish-looking lead, is a misfiled US company). Freshness broke the tie: **Distance Technologies** (FI, seed: Cerebras, 13 Nov), **Dalip** (NO, pre-seed: Arondite, 12 Nov), **RiACT** (DK, seed: Tekever, 10 Nov); the Swedish candidates' doors are from June. Oura and Verda leave the demo: not seed stage (Oura has the only tier-2 log, Cerebras via Atreides). Their frozen reports are removed; `/for/oura` and `/for/verda` still build live from the pool.
50. **Own-domain sources rank PRIMARY for entries too** (Avanza's investor-relations page), as they already did for rounds.

## v3 (north star: "Faro tells you which companies just got a reason to partner (new money, new hires, new acquisitions), why they need you, and who can introduce you.")

51. **Entrants pass stopped, warm-path searches into entrants stopped, market-entry searches stopped.** Market entries stay as timed doors (tier 3). Serves the north star: calls go to reasons that come with an introducer.
52. **One call per company for every new reason.** `SIGNAL_QUERY` (src/lib/signals.ts) is the profile query plus `PARTICIPATES_IN_CORPORATE_EVENT` (the link itself carries role, `event_date` and sources) plus `numerical_observations: {ObservableProperty: [<Number of employees>]}` (found by introspection; the same metric id on every company; values come with time and origin). It yields new executives (day-precise `valid_since`, still in role, sourced; 180 days), headcount jumps (latest point ≥ +5 people and ≥ +30% on the one before, sourced by its origin; 365 days, as registries report yearly), completed acquisitions as acquirer (180 days; the target named with one more call when budget allows) and financings as issuer (new money, 120 days).
53. **Ranking v3:** 1 any reason + warm path + shared event, 2 any reason + warm path, 3 market entry, 4 another reason without a warm path. Rounds without a warm path were hidden before; now they, new hires and acquisitions show in tier 4 with "Find the right person →", because the north star names the reason first and an introducer second. Newest reason wins per company.
54. **No giants as headlines.** A giant (listed, multinational, ≥ 1,000 people, or ≥ 20 investors in Cala) can appear but never leads the log or the first look; the first non-giant takes its place.
55. **No weapons makers in the demos.** Demo reports are frozen with `noWeapons`: a defence-coded company with military wording in its description, claim or source (Tekever, Arondite) is left out. The engine keeps them for ordinary users; it's a demo rule, as asked.
56. **Keyrings walked for the new reasons:** the demo startups' investors. Maki.vc and Butterfly Ventures keyrings come from `data/dataset.json` (free); others cost one portfolio call each. Public agencies and global funds (Business Finland, GV) aren't walked: weak introducers, huge keyrings. Nordic companies first, round-robin across the demo startups, hard cap on calls actually sent (`--calls`).
